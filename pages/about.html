About Me

A little about me.

Hello, my name is Emma! Welcome to my little corner of the internet.

I enjoy using computational and statistical methods to tackle problems in the physical sciences, particularly those related to climate and environmental systems. In the long term, I hope to build a research career focused on using computational approaches to address challenges in these areas.

I completed a BSc (Hons) in Computational Physics at the University of Edinburgh, including a year abroad at UCLA, followed by an MPhil in Data Intensive Science at the University of Cambridge. I am now about to begin a DPhil as part of the Intelligent Earth CDT at the University of Oxford.

During my first year, I will take courses in machine learning, statistics and climate physics while exploring different research areas. This will give me the opportunity to develop a clearer research direction and make a more informed decision about the focus of my DPhil.

Across academic and industry settings, I have worked on a variety of projects, from modelling bacterial motion in fluid flows to analysing climate variability. What I enjoy most is addressing problems where scientific understanding directly shapes computational modelling. Applying physics to develop and refine models not only advances the code but also uncovers new insights that increases our collective knowledge.

I like to think life is more than academics, even if study has taken much of my time recently. In my free moments, I enjoy playing piano, baking and reading. I especially love being outdoors, gardening, wild swimming, scuba diving and hill walking. I love being in nature and find a sense of peace being outside. Of course these moments are always better with my friends and family, whose support and joy improve my life in every way.
