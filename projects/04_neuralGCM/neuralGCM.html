NeuralGCM: Hybrid Physics-AI for Weather and Climate. With Additional Tropical Cyclone Case Studies

In 2026, I completed my MPhil project asking whether NeuralGCM is a good weather and climate model. NeuralGCM takes a hybrid approach, combining a physical dynamical core with machine learning components. This aims to retain the physical grounding and interpretability of numerical weather prediction while benefiting from the speed and forecast accuracy of data driven models. My project was supervised by Professor Sebastian Schemm, Dr Simon Driscoll and Pritthijit Nath.

The project had two parts. First, I reproduced results from the original NeuralGCM paper using forecast accuracy metrics, Hurricane Laura and annual tropical cyclone tracks. I then extended the evaluation through three tropical cyclone case studies, testing whether NeuralGCM captured the correct tracks, intensity and distinctive dynamics. Gulab and Shaheen tested re-intensification, Faraji tested a curved track, and Seroja and Odette tested the Fujiwhara interaction between two cyclones.

The original paper's main findings were broadly reproduced, although some accuracy metrics were weaker. In the additional case studies, NeuralGCM partly captured the cyclone tracks but consistently underestimated intensity. The deterministic models captured Faraji's curvature and the Seroja and Odette Fujiwhara interaction, while the re-intensification of Gulab into Shaheen was missed. Overall, NeuralGCM is a strong hybrid physics and AI model, but it still struggles with cyclone intensity and complex dynamics.
